# REVERSE SHELL — Game Bible & Story Outline

**Version 0.3 — October 23, 2026**

> *Sunlight through a broken roof. Wind in the grass that grew over the road. Somewhere in the green, a machine is still keeping watch.*

This document is the creative source of truth for Reverse Shell. Established decisions are treated as canon; unresolved ideas remain proposals until deliberately confirmed.

---

## 1. Game Overview

- **Genre:** Narrative exploration RPG with tactical card-based combat.
- **Visual direction:** HD-2D pixel art, combining detailed pixel characters with dimensional environments, atmospheric lighting, and environmental storytelling.
- **Combat influences:** Reverse: 1999's card merging and tactical card play, adapted into an original system.
- **Core experience:** Explore a beautiful world left behind by humanity, encounter machines that have outlived their makers, build a small party, and discover what remains of human identity after death.

### Design pillars
1. **Beauty in decay.** The world is quiet, overgrown, and sunlit. Its melancholy should leave room for wonder.
2. **Every card is a decision.** Card play, merging, AP management, and Ultimate timing reward tactical thinking.
3. **Small kindnesses matter.** Characters reveal themselves through choices, companionship, and how they treat others.
4. **Mystery through discovery.** Records, environments, enemies, and conflicting beliefs reveal the past gradually.
5. **No easy answer to humanity.** The game explores whether a mechanical continuation of a human is still that person without dictating the player's conclusion.

## 2. The World and Its History

### The environmental collapse
Earth became increasingly uninhabitable. Different regions suffered different forms of environmental catastrophe: extreme cold, flooding and submerged landscapes, volcanic instability, and other hostile conditions.

Mechanical bodies began as a practical response. They could survive environments that biological humans could no longer endure.

The wealthy and powerful transformed first. As conditions worsened, mechanical conversion became a necessity for more people. Access to the technology created social divisions.

Eventually, survival was no longer its only purpose. Humanity became obsessed with immortality.

### Reverse Shell
Reverse Shell is a procedure intended to preserve a person indefinitely by scanning and recording their brain into a **Soul Card**, then placing that recorded identity into a mechanical body.

The procedure is permanent. The original biological host dies, and the process cannot be reversed.

The mechanical continuation inherits the recorded memories and personality and believes itself to be the person who underwent the procedure. Whether the original consciousness or soul truly survives the transfer remains unknown.

The opening presents Reverse Shell through an optimistic public-information recording. The procedure is advertised as humanity's best idea for everlasting life, emphasizing the word **EVERLASTING**. It should initially sound reassuring rather than overtly sinister.

The truth behind the procedure emerges gradually.

### The war and humanity's extinction
Environmental collapse, inequality, and disagreements over humanity's future contributed to conflict. War became increasingly dependent on mechanical bodies, which could fight through damage and endure conditions that would incapacitate biological soldiers.

The conflict continued long after its original purpose had been lost.

Humanity ultimately destroyed itself. The war and the pursuit of immortality contributed to that outcome, but the precise chronology and balance of causes remain open.

The player should piece together the truth from incomplete records, ruined infrastructure, and the beliefs of surviving machine people.

## 3. Soul Cards and Cores

### Soul Cards
Soul Cards are vessels that can bind with a soul. They are not inherently souls themselves.

Original Soul Cards contain recorded brain data and identity information from humans who once lived. Wiped or blank Soul Cards can be reused.

The central uncertainty is whether recorded identity preserves the actual soul or merely produces a convincing continuation of the person.

### Cores
Cores power and sustain mechanical bodies. They are distinct from Soul Cards and can deteriorate or fail.

The protagonist's Core system is also tied to combat customization. Equipping or replacing Cores changes abilities, stats, and combat style. The relationship between this equipment system and the protagonist's elemental resonance progression must remain consistent as the mechanics are finalized.

### Elemental resonances
The protagonist begins with one physical Core. After defeating the electric dragon in Chapter One, they obtain a blank Soul Card. The card binds to and is absorbed by the protagonist's Core, unlocking **Electric resonance**. The Soul Card is consumed rather than retained as a separate item.

Absorbing further guardian-associated Soul Cards can permanently unlock additional resonances. The protagonist can switch among unlocked resonances.

An active resonance changes the element and effects of the protagonist's innate cards and determines which resonance-specific Ultimate is available.

The exact relationship between resonance progression, replaceable Core equipment, and any retained identity data remains an open design question.

## 4. The Organization and the Soul Creation Project
An organization of mechanical people hunts powerful mechanical creatures for valuable resources, especially Soul Cards. It needs these resources to sustain its population and create new machine people.

Its members are former humans living in mechanical bodies and can bind with Soul Cards.

The organization can manufacture bodies and manipulate recorded data, but it cannot create a new soul. Its attempt to manufacture new life—the **Soul Creation Project**—has failed.

Only the leadership and a small group of researchers know the full truth. Most of the population believes the project is progressing.

The leadership conceals the failure because it fears that the truth would cause despair, anarchy, and social fracture. Its motives are not purely malicious: its members want their people to survive, even if they are maintaining that hope through deception.

The exact process of creating new machine people and the way Soul Cards are allocated remain undecided.

## 5. Guardians and the Regions
Powerful mechanical creatures guard Soul Cards. Their bodies have adapted to the extreme environments of their respective regions, producing distinct elemental identities.

Each region has a signature element, reflected in its environment, guardian, and associated Soul Card. Elements influence exploration challenges as well as combat.

The first confirmed guardian is an **electric dragon**. It guards a Soul Card but has no soul of its own and cannot use the card it protects.

Defeating guardians can have different consequences. Some shut down peacefully; others leave lasting environmental changes.

The origins of the guardians, their reasons for protecting Soul Cards, and whether every guardian follows the same pattern remain open.

### Environmental interaction
Resonances can affect certain environmental interactions outside combat, but they are not universal keys for every puzzle.

Switching resonance is free outside combat. In combat, switching is limited or costly; the exact AP cost and restrictions are not yet established.

## 6. Characters and Party

### The protagonist
- The protagonist is a robot who awakens alone in the ruins after the Reverse Shell procedure fails.
- The player names the protagonist during the opening.
- They initially lack a complete understanding of their identity and the world. Fragmented memories and discoveries gradually reveal their connection to humanity and the Reverse Shell procedure.
- The protagonist is actually a human continuation in a mechanical body, but they do not initially know this.

### Companions
- The protagonist begins Chapter One alone and meets a companion in Chapter Two.
- The party is intended to be small, with a maximum of four recruited characters and three active in battle, subject to final confirmation of the roster.
- Party members have their own innate cards, Ultimate/Soul Card abilities, and changeable cards. The protagonist has a distinct Core-based customization and resonance system.
- Previous character names and faction labels from the older development bible are provisional, not binding canon. Character identities, names, and backstories should be original.

## 7. Combat System

### Core combat loop
Combat is turn-based and card-driven. Players manage AP, play cards, merge matching cards to raise their rank, observe enemy intent, exploit weaknesses, and build toward powerful Ultimates.

Card merging is a gameplay convention and does not require an in-world explanation.

There is no dedicated Break gauge. Tactical openings are expressed through existing systems such as enemy intent, card ranks, interruptions, defense disruption, and stagger effects.

### Innate cards and resonance
The protagonist's innate cards transform into distinct elemental attacks and abilities based on the currently active resonance. The difference should go beyond changing damage color.

Each resonance has its own Ultimate, with a distinct effect and tactical role. Some Ultimates emphasize damage; others may heal, protect, or buff allies.

### Ultimate charge — established rules
- **Individual charge:** Every party member builds their own Ultimate charge independently.
- **Charge generation:** Playing cards and taking actions builds Ultimate charge.
- **Multiple Ultimates:** Multiple characters can use their charged Ultimates in the same turn, subject to normal action rules.
- **Between battles:** Ultimate charge fully resets after every battle. Characters begin each new encounter with zero Ultimate charge.
- **Protagonist's Ultimate:** The protagonist's active resonance determines their available Ultimate.
- **Allies' Ultimates:** Allies have their own Ultimate/Soul Card abilities.

The exact charge threshold and the amount contributed by individual actions remain undecided.

### AP and card merging
AP governs actions during combat. Players decide how to spend AP, when to merge cards, and when to use available abilities.

Merging raises card rank and strengthens or improves its effect. The precise merge rules and card-pool architecture should follow the current implementation unless deliberately redesigned.

### Weaknesses and enemy information
Elemental weaknesses increase damage and can create tactical openings.

Players discover weaknesses through experimentation and observing enemy reactions rather than through an automatic scanner reveal.

Once a machine type's weakness is discovered, it is permanently recorded in enemy information or the bestiary. Whether individual variants can have different weaknesses remains open.

### Optical scanner
An optical scanning device introduced in Chapter One can detect traces, weak points, and materials from powerful creatures and reveal hidden paths.

It is useful for locating and harvesting resources. It does not automatically reveal elemental weaknesses.

### Exploration encounters and recovery
- Visible machines can trigger battles when touched.
- Tall grass may eventually host random encounters.
- Access Points serve as recovery stations and are intended to become save points.
- HP carries over between consecutive battles.
- Ultimate charge does not carry over; it fully resets after each battle.

## 8. Progression and Customization
The legacy implementation includes the following systems, whose exact integration with the evolving lore should be reviewed as needed:
- Character levels and stat growth.
- Card upgrades using salvaged Parts.
- Four Part slots per robot: weapon, armor, chip, and Core.
- Passive effects attached to equipment or upgraded cards.
- Recruitment and party composition.
- New cards and new districts as long-term progression.

The protagonist's Core equipment system is confirmed to affect abilities, stats, and combat style. Details that overlap with resonance absorption should not be assumed settled until reconciled explicitly.

## 9. Chapter Outline
The later chapter names and events below are a working structure, not all confirmed story canon.

### Prologue — The Promise of Everlasting Life
An old public-information recording introduces Reverse Shell as a permanent path to everlasting life. Its tone is hopeful and reassuring, emphasizing EVERLASTING.

The procedure fails, and the protagonist awakens in the ruins.

### Chapter One — The Overgrown City
- The protagonist awakens alone in a sunlit, overgrown city.
- They explore the ruins, experience fragments of unfamiliar memories, and encounter the traces of an old world.
- They discover an optical scanner made by the organization to locate and harvest resources from powerful mechanical creatures. An organization member confronts them and demands its return. A fight follows, after which the member retreats.
- The scanner reveals a hidden route into a cave, tunnel, or buried passage. There, the protagonist encounters an electric dragon guarding a blank Soul Card.
- After defeating the dragon, the protagonist takes the card. It binds with and is absorbed by the Core, unlocking Electric resonance.
- The chapter ends with a new mystery rather than a complete explanation of the protagonist's identity.

### Chapter Two — A Stranger in the Ruins
The protagonist meets their first companion. The encounter begins to establish trust, companionship, and different perspectives on the ruined world.

Exact events and the companion's identity remain open.

### Chapter Three — The Organization's Reach
The organization becomes a more direct presence. The protagonist learns more about its resource hunts and its attempts to preserve a population of mechanical people.

Specific confrontations and revelations remain open.

### Chapter Four — The Buried Past
The protagonist finds deeper evidence of the old world, the war, and Reverse Shell. Records should complicate the player's understanding rather than provide a single authoritative explanation for every mystery.

Specific events remain open.

### Chapter Five — The Cinderworks
A vast industrial complex stands beside a volcanic fissure, surrounded by molten lava and machinery built to withstand extreme heat.

The facility contains failed experiments, discarded artificial bodies, laboratories, and attempts to manufacture Soul Cards and Cores.

Here, the protagonist confronts the distinction between making a body and creating life. Humanity could reproduce mechanical forms and recorded data but could not manufacture a genuine soul.

The Cinderworks may reveal the truth the organization's leadership is hiding about the Soul Creation Project. Its exact chapter placement and sequence of revelations remain subject to revision.

### Chapter Six — The Cost of a Future
The protagonist's discoveries force the party to confront the cost of survival, the limits of the organization's plans, and what kind of future is worth preserving.

The precise events remain open.

### Final Chapter — The Last Gift
The current thematic direction is for the protagonist to give up their Core to save someone else.

The act should embody a choice to give one's life or future for another person, contrasting the old world's obsession with everlasting existence.

The recipient, mechanics of the sacrifice, permanence of the protagonist's death, and final scene are not yet decided.

## 10. Visual Direction

### Mood and palette
The world is quiet, hazy, and sunlit. Desaturated greens and warm concrete sit beneath a soft blue-white sky. Long shafts of light, drifting leaves, pollen, and dust emphasize the slow reclamation of human spaces by nature.

Machines provide stronger visual accents through rusted plating and glowing red-orange eyes.

| Use | Color | Hex |
|---|---|---|
| Sky | Washed blue | `#85A8CC` |
| Haze | Pale cream | `#E0E0CC` |
| Concrete | Sun-bleached gray-beige | `#A59C88` |
| Foliage | Moss through sunlit green | `#24452A` `#356A34` `#55913F` `#8DBF57` |
| Rust | Machine plating | `#94622F` |
| Threat | Machine eye red | `#FF4729` |
| UI | Sepia paper / dark ink | `#D9D1B5` / `#454033` |

The environment stays soft and low-contrast. Stronger saturation is reserved for threats, important powers, and effects that need to read clearly.

### HD-2D implementation notes
- One Unity unit equals one tile.
- Tile art uses 16×16 pixel-art source cells stored at 4× resolution, with point filtering.
- Existing tile atlas: `Art/Tiles/ruins.png`. Tile-generation script: `Tools/TileForge/ruins_atlas.py`.
- Character sprites use 32×48 pixel LPC compositions generated by `Tools/SpriteForge/lpc_compose.py`. Animation directions include idle, walk, slash, spellcast, and hurt.
- Camera: approximately 28° field of view, pitched around 38°, following from the south.
- Lighting: dappled sunlight, low green ambient light, and haze. Avoid artificial point lights unless justified by a scene.
- Post-processing: gentle bloom, warm slightly desaturated grading, light vignette, and subtle grain.
- Avoid depth-of-field blur that makes sprites unclear.

### UI principles
Readability takes priority. Use large numbers, concise labels, and clear card effects.

Overworld UI uses sepia paper panels and dark ink. Cards use a dark body with an owner-colored frame and clearly stated effects. Damage numbers should communicate meaning consistently.

## 11. Tone Guide

**Aim for:**
- Quiet melancholy.
- Small acts of kindness.
- Curiosity about machines and their inner lives.
- Beauty in decay.
- Conflicting beliefs about what makes someone human.
- Hope that does not depend on pretending the past was good.

**Avoid:**
- Grimdark despair for its own sake.
- Treating machines as automatically soulless or automatically human.
- Explaining every mystery through one exposition dump.
- Copying another game's characters, plot, or signature story beats.

**Writing test:** Would this line feel natural spoken quietly in a sunlit, empty street?

## 12. Open Decisions
These points are intentionally unresolved:
- How Core equipment interacts mechanically with permanent resonance unlocks.
- Whether absorbing Soul Cards restores memories or other fragments of the protagonist's identity.
- The full elemental roster and its final names and matchup rules.
- The exact AP cost or limitations for changing resonance during combat.
- Ultimate charge thresholds and per-action charge values.
- Exact card-merging rules and the final card architecture.
- The complete roster, character names, and companion stories.
- The origins and purpose of the guardians.
- The exact method the organization uses to create machine people.
- The chapter placement and details of the Soul Creation Project reveal.
- The recipient and consequences of the protagonist's final sacrifice.
- Whether the protagonist's final sacrifice is permanent.
- The final names of factions, districts, and other legacy placeholders.

## Guiding Rule
Reverse Shell should not tell the player that mechanical people are—or are not—human. It should present the history, the contradictions, the kindnesses, the cruelties, and the choices, then allow the player to decide what those things mean.

## 13. Implementation notes (non-canon)
This section maps the canon above onto the current code. Nothing here is story canon.

### Names in code
- **Electric resonance** is the code element `Volt`. The protagonist's starting **Physical** Core is `Afflatus.None` (no element; neutral in every matchup). The other code elements (Fire, Toxin, Earth, Light, Void) are legacy placeholders for future resonances; their final names and matchups are open (§12).
- **Ultimate** is shown as "Ult" (code name: Moxie; Ritual cards are Ultimate cards). Ult charge already resets every battle.
- The protagonist's asset is `Char_MC`; the player's chosen name replaces "MC" everywhere it is shown.

### Legacy placeholders (debug only)
- **Heal Bot, Lucy and Blue**, the old crew, are not part of the story. They live in the **Training Center** (the old Mossline Station test map), reached only through the **Training Mode** setting.
- Older faction and district names (the Bunker, the Resistance, the Hollow Choir, Mossline Station, the Sunken Arcade…) are not canon.

### Chapter One — provisional decisions (awaiting approval)
- The organization is never named in-game. Its member in Chapter One is called **Collector**, a job title rather than a name.
- The Chapter One city is the reworked **City Ruins** map, with a buried tunnel and the dragon's chamber as two small extra areas.
- Record texts, memory fragments, the Collector's dialogue and the dragon's build-up are first drafts.
- **Ending mystery (draft):** after the blank Soul Card binds, the scanner registers a second trace carrying the same signature as the protagonist's own Core, leading away from the city. It hints without explaining.
- The protagonist's Electric kit (cards and Ultimate) and Chapter One enemy stats are first drafts for balance.
- All Chapter One enemies use the red Rust Sentry sprite as placeholder art, with their own names.
